## What is a stop loss

A stop loss is an order you place with your broker to exit a trade if the price moves against you. Write the definition in two or three clear sentences. The "Quick answer" box above the article is taken from the `description` field, so keep that one sharp.

## Why it matters

> Example: You buy a share at ₹100 and set a stop loss at ₹95. If the price falls to ₹95, the trade closes with a ₹5 loss per share.

## Types of stop loss

:::compare
Type | How it works | Best for
Fixed stop | Set at one price | Beginners
Trailing stop | Moves up as price rises | Trending trades
Time stop | Exit after N days | Swing trades
:::

## Key numbers to remember

:::facts
Common risk per trade: 1-2% of capital
Usual placement: Below support for a buy
Order type: Stop loss / SL-M
:::

## How to set a stop loss, step by step

:::steps
Pick your entry price
Find the nearest support level
Place the stop just below it
Size your position so the loss is within your risk limit
:::

## Pros and cons

:::proscons
+ Limits loss on every trade
+ Removes emotion from the exit
- Can trigger on a short spike
- Gaps can fill you below your price
:::

:::note
Stop loss does not guarantee the exact exit price. In a fast market or a gap, your order can fill at a worse price.
:::

## Common mistakes

- Setting the stop too tight and getting stopped out by normal moves
- Moving the stop further away after the trade goes wrong
- Trading without any stop
