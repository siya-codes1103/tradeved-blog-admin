## What Is IPO Allotment?

When a privately owned company raises capital from the public for the first time, it issues an Initial Public Offering (IPO). Once you place a bid during the subscription window, the company and its designated registrar review the applications and distribute the shares. This mechanism is called **IPO Allotment**.

If market demand matches or falls below the issue size, all valid applicants receive their applied shares. When an issue receives bids far higher than the available shares—known as **oversubscription**—allotment follows structured regulatory frameworks established by SEBI.

> Example: If a company offers 100,000 shares to retail investors but receives bids for 1,000,000 shares, the retail category is 10x oversubscribed. The company cannot issue more shares than planned, meaning shares must be allocated systematically via a computerised draw.

## Retail vs HNI Investor Categories

Different investor categories operate under distinct allotment mechanisms:

:::compare
Category | Investment Threshold | Allocation Method Under Oversubscription
Retail Individual Investor (RII) | Up to ₹2 Lakh | Computerised Lottery (Minimum 1 Lot)
Small Non-Institutional (sHNI) | ₹2 Lakh to ₹10 Lakh | Draw of Lots (Specified Lot Size)
Big Non-Institutional (bHNI) | Above ₹10 Lakh | Proportionate Allocation
:::

## Key Metrics to Remember

:::facts
Retail Quota: Typically 35% in standard book-built issues
Maximum Retail Cap: ₹2,00,000 per application
Lottery Priority: SEBI mandates allocating 1 minimum lot per selected applicant
UPI Mandate: Unallotted application funds are released automatically
:::

## Steps in the IPO Allotment Process

:::steps
Submit your bid through your broker and authorize the UPI mandate before the cut-off time.
The registrar aggregates all bids and eliminates invalid or duplicate PAN submissions.
If oversubscribed, a randomized computerized lottery selects valid retail bids.
Successful applicants receive shares in their Demat account, while blocked bank funds are debited.
Unsuccessful applicants receive automated UPI mandate revocation, releasing blocked funds.
:::

## Pros and Cons of Participating in IPOs

:::proscons
+ Provides entry into growing companies at the foundational issue price
+ High probability of listing premiums during buoyant market conditions
- High oversubscription significantly lowers individual allotment probability
- Companies with speculative valuations may list below the issue price
:::

:::note
Submitting multiple bids under the same PAN number through different brokers (such as applying once via Zerodha and once via Groww) causes both applications to be flagged as duplicates and rejected. Always maintain one application per PAN in the retail category.
:::

## Common Application Mistakes

- Bidding below the upper price band instead of selecting the Cut-Off Price.
- Missing the UPI payment mandate authorization window.
- Relying exclusively on Grey Market Premium (GMP) indicators without analyzing financial statements.

## Conclusion

IPO allotment during high-demand offerings is governed by mathematical probability. Rather than chasing short-term listing momentum, evaluate the company's valuation multiples, debt obligations, and peer comparison before committing capital.
